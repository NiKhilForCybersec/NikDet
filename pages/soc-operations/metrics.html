Common Metrics Problems

Most SOC metrics programs fail not because of bad intentions, but because of these common pitfalls:

❌ Problem 1: Measuring Activity, Not Outcomes

What happens: SOC tracks "tickets closed per analyst" as primary metric.

Result: Analysts rush through alerts, close things without proper investigation, quality drops.

Real example: Analyst closes 50 tickets/day, looks great on paper. 3 months later, breach discovered - one of those "closed" tickets was the initial access that was missed.

❌ Problem 2: Vanity Metrics That Don't Help

What happens: Weekly reports show "500,000 alerts blocked" and "99.9% uptime".

Result: Leadership thinks security is great. But... were any real threats detected? Nobody knows.

Real example: Security report shows 2 million "threats blocked" - turns out 99.8% are spam emails, not threats.

❌ Problem 3: Metrics That Punish Good Behavior

What happens: Analysts measured on "time to close" - faster = better.

Result: Analysts don't escalate (takes longer), don't investigate thoroughly, close as FP when unsure.

Real example: Analyst could escalate suspicious alert (right thing) but closes as FP instead because escalation "hurts their numbers."

❌ Problem 4: Gaming the Metrics

What happens: MTTA target is 15 minutes. Analysts learn to "acknowledge" immediately without actually looking.

Result: MTTA looks great (3 min average!), but actual investigation doesn't start for hours.

Metrics Gaming Examples

If you measure it, people will optimize for it. Here's how metrics get gamed:

Gaming Example: Mean Time to Acknowledge (MTTA)

Metric:MTTA target < 15 minutes
How it's gamed:Analyst opens alert, clicks "acknowledge", moves on. Comes back to actually look at it hours later.
Dashboard shows:MTTA: 4 minutes ✓
Reality:Actual investigation starts 2 hours later

Fix: Measure time to MEANINGFUL action (first query run, first note added, classification decision)

Gaming Example: Tickets Closed Per Day

Metric:Target: 30 tickets/analyst/day
How it's gamed:Close obvious FPs instantly, bulk close similar alerts, avoid complex investigations
Dashboard shows:Average: 35 tickets/day ✓
Reality:True positives getting closed as FP, complex threats missed

Fix: Track closure quality (audit random sample), separate simple vs complex tickets

Gaming Example: True Positive Rate

Metric:Target: >50% True Positive rate
How it's gamed:Disable noisy rules, only keep rules that rarely fire
Dashboard shows:TP Rate: 68% ✓
Reality:Detection coverage dropped by 40%, now blind to common attacks

Fix: Track TP rate AND coverage (which techniques are detected)

What Actually Matters

Instead of vanity metrics, focus on outcomes:

✓ Did we detect real threats?

  • Metric: Number of confirmed True Positives requiring action
  • Why: This is the actual job - finding real threats
  • Target: Track trend, not absolute number

✓ How fast did we respond to real threats?

  • Metric: Time from TRUE POSITIVE alert to containment
  • Why: Speed matters only for real incidents
  • Target: < 4 hours for High severity incidents

✓ What did we miss?

  • Metric: Incidents discovered by someone other than SOC (IT, user, third party)
  • Why: This is your detection gap
  • Target: < 10% of incidents discovered externally

✓ How much noise are we dealing with?

  • Metric: Alert-to-Incident ratio (how many alerts per real incident)
  • Why: Shows detection quality and analyst fatigue risk
  • Target: < 100:1 (100 alerts per real incident)

✓ Can analysts keep up?

  • Metric: Alert backlog trend (growing or shrinking)
  • Why: Growing backlog = analysts overwhelmed
  • Target: Flat or declining backlog

Time-Based Metrics

MetricWhat It MeasuresRealistic TargetWatch Out For
MTTD
(Mean Time to Detect)		 Time from attack start to alert firing Varies by attack type Hard to measure - you need to know when attack actually started
MTTA
(Mean Time to Acknowledge)		 Time from alert to analyst picks it up < 15 min (Critical)
< 1 hr (High)		 Easy to game - measure meaningful action instead
MTTR
(Mean Time to Respond)		 Time from alert to containment complete < 4 hrs (High severity) Only meaningful for True Positives
Dwell Time How long attacker was present before detection < 7 days Only measurable after incident is investigated

Quality Metrics

MetricWhat It MeasuresTargetHow to Calculate
True Positive Rate % of investigated alerts that were real > 30-50% TPs / (TPs + FPs)
Escalation Quality % of escalations that were appropriate > 80% Valid escalations / Total escalations
Detection Coverage % of MITRE techniques with detections > 60% Covered techniques / Total relevant techniques
Missed Incidents Incidents found by others, not SOC < 10% External discoveries / Total incidents

Reporting to Leadership

What Executives Actually Care About

They AskThey MeanWhat to Show
"Are we secure?" Are we at risk? Will we be breached? Real incidents detected, response times, what we're blind to
"How's the SOC performing?" Is my investment paying off? Threats stopped, incidents contained, comparison to past
"What do you need?" What will reduce risk further? Coverage gaps, capacity issues, tool limitations

Monthly Report Template

Executive Summary Format

SECURITY OPERATIONS MONTHLY REPORT
January 2025

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
-----------------
This month we detected and contained 47 security incidents, a 15% 
increase from December. Key wins:
• Stopped 1 ransomware attempt before encryption (saved ~$2M estimated)
• Detected compromised vendor account within 2 hours
• Zero data breaches or business impact

CONCERNS
• Alert volume increased 30% - approaching team capacity
• Two detection gaps identified during purple team exercise

KEY METRICS
-----------
| Metric                | Jan 2025 | Dec 2024 | Target | Status |
|----------------------|----------|----------|--------|--------|
| Incidents Detected   | 47       | 41       | -      | ▲      |
| Avg Response Time    | 2.1 hrs  | 2.4 hrs  | < 4 hrs| ✓      |
| Missed Incidents     | 0        | 1        | < 10%  | ✓      |
| Detection Coverage   | 67%      | 65%      | > 60%  | ✓      |

RECOMMENDATIONS
---------------
1. Add 1 L1 analyst to handle increased volume
2. Implement detection for identified gaps (T1055, T1134)
3. Schedule quarterly purple team exercises

⚠️ Avoid These Reporting Mistakes

  • Don't lead with vanity metrics - "Blocked 5 million threats" means nothing
  • Don't hide bad news - Missed incidents are learning opportunities
  • Don't use jargon - Executives don't know what MTTA is
  • Don't provide data without context - "47 incidents" vs "47 incidents, up 15%, handled with zero business impact"
