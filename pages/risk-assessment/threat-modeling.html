Threat Modeling Process

1
Identify Assets

What are you protecting? Crown jewels, critical systems, data.

2
Profile Threat Actors

Who would attack you? What are their motivations and capabilities?

3
Map Attack Vectors

How could they attack? Entry points, techniques, tools.

4
Identify Controls & Gaps

What stops them? Where are detection gaps?

5
Prioritize & Act

Build detections for highest-risk scenarios first.

Attack Path Analysis

Example Attack Path: Ransomware

1. Initial Access
   └── Phishing email with macro → T1566.001
       └── Detection: Email gateway, endpoint macro block

2. Execution
   └── PowerShell download → T1059.001
       └── Detection: PowerShell logging, EDR

3. Persistence
   └── Scheduled task → T1053.005
       └── Detection: Task creation monitoring

4. Credential Access
   └── LSASS dumping → T1003.001
       └── Detection: LSASS access monitoring

5. Lateral Movement
   └── RDP with stolen creds → T1021.001
       └── Detection: Unusual RDP, behavior analytics

6. Impact
   └── File encryption → T1486
       └── Detection: Mass file modification, known ransomware

Threat Model Output for SOC

  • Priority threat actors: Top 3-5 actors most likely to target you
  • Priority TTPs: Techniques to focus detection on
  • Attack paths: Most likely paths to crown jewels
  • Detection gaps: Where you can't see attacks
  • Detection roadmap: Prioritized list of detections to build
