Why Security Controls Matter

Connecting security controls to real-world attack scenarios, business impacts, and outcomes. Understand the "why" behind every security decision.

Real-World Attack Scenarios

Scenario 1: No MFA → Account Takeover → Wire Fraud

What Happens Without MFA
StageWhat HappenedControl That Would Have Stopped It
1. Credential theftCFO clicked phishing link, entered credentials on fake M365 loginSecurity awareness training, email filtering
2. Account accessAttacker logged in with stolen password (no MFA)MFA would have blocked here
3. ReconnaissanceAttacker read emails, learned payment processesImpossible travel detection, session monitoring
4. Inbox ruleCreated rule to hide replies from vendorInbox rule monitoring, CASB alerts
5. Wire fraudSent email to AP as CFO requesting $2.3M wire to "new" vendor accountEmail DLP, BEC detection, callback verification process
6. Money goneWire sent, funds unrecoverable—

Business Impact: $2.3M direct loss, CFO terminated, insurance claim denied (no MFA = negligence), regulatory investigation, stock price drop.

MFA Cost: ~$3/user/month. MFA would have prevented: $2.3M+ loss.

Scenario 2: No Network Segmentation → Ransomware Everywhere

What Happens With Flat Network
StageWhat HappenedControl That Would Have Stopped It
1. Initial accessUser opened malicious attachment, malware executedEmail security, EDR, user training
2. DiscoveryMalware scanned network, found all 2,000 devices (flat network)Segmentation would have limited visibility
3. Lateral movementUsed SMB to spread to file servers, domain controllerFirewall rules between segments
4. Privilege escalationDumped credentials from DC, got domain adminPAM, credential guard, DC isolation
5. DeploymentPushed ransomware to all 2,000 devices simultaneouslyMicrosegmentation, GPO restrictions
6. EncryptionAll systems encrypted including backups (on same network)Backup network isolation

Business Impact: 3 weeks downtime, $15M recovery costs, $5M ransom paid (no backups), lost contracts, regulatory fines, 50 layoffs.

Segmentation Cost: ~$200K implementation. Would have limited impact to: 1 department, 50 devices, 2 days recovery.

StageWhat HappenedControl That Would Have Stopped It

DLP Cost: ~$50K/year. Would have: Blocked exfiltration, created evidence for legal action.

Answering Common Objections

"It's Too Expensive"

ObjectionResponseData Point
"We can't afford MFA"You can't afford NOT to have it. One BEC incident costs more than 10 years of MFA.Average BEC loss: $125,000. MFA cost: ~$10,000/year.
"EDR is too expensive for our size"Ransomware doesn't care about company size. SMBs are actually targeted more.43% of cyber attacks target SMBs. Average SMB breach: $2.98M.
"We can't justify SIEM cost"The cost of NOT detecting breaches is much higher (regulatory fines, dwell time damage).Average dwell time without SIEM: 287 days. With SIEM: 30-60 days.

"It's Too Disruptive"

ObjectionResponseMitigation
"MFA will slow users down"Modern MFA takes 5 seconds. Users adapt in days. A breach disrupts for months.Use push notifications, Windows Hello, SSO to reduce prompts.
"DLP will block legitimate work"Start with monitoring only. Tune before blocking. Create exceptions for valid workflows.Phased rollout, business process exceptions, user training.
"Segmentation will break applications"Implement in monitor mode first. Document required flows. Enable blocking gradually.Traffic analysis before changes, application owner involvement.

"We've Never Been Breached"

ObjectionResponseReality Check
"We're not a target"Everyone is a target. Automated attacks don't discriminate. You have money, data, or access to someone who does.SMBs face 350,000+ attacks per year on average.
"We've been fine without it"You might be breached and not know it. Average dwell time is 200+ days. Absence of evidence isn't evidence of absence.60% of SMBs that suffer a breach go out of business within 6 months.
"Our industry isn't targeted"Every industry is targeted. Healthcare, manufacturing, education, retail - all have major breach incidents.Top targeted industries change yearly. No one is exempt.
