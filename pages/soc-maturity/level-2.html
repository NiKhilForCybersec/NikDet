Level 2: Managed

Basic processes documented, dedicated staff assigned, and foundational monitoring in place. The organization recognizes security as a distinct function.

Level 2 Characteristics

DomainTypical State
BusinessSecurity recognized as necessary, basic budget allocated
People1-3 dedicated security staff or MSSP engagement
ProcessBasic playbooks for common scenarios, some documentation
TechnologySIEM deployed, EDR on critical systems, basic alerting
Services8x5 or limited 24x7 monitoring, reactive investigation

Common Level 2 Challenges

  • Alert fatigue from too many false positives
  • Limited coverage—not all systems monitored
  • Processes exist but not consistently followed
  • Single points of failure (key person dependency)
  • Difficulty measuring effectiveness
  • Reactive rather than proactive posture

Priority Improvements to Reach Level 3

PriorityActionWhy It Matters
1Expand log coverage to all critical systemsEliminate blind spots
2Tune detections to reduce false positivesFocus analyst time on real threats
3Implement tiered SOC structure (L1/L2)Efficient alert handling
4Create comprehensive playbook libraryConsistent, quality response
5Begin tracking basic metrics (MTTD, MTTR)Measure to improve
6Cross-train staffReduce key person risk

Key Metrics to Track

  • Alert volume - Total alerts per day/week
  • True positive rate - % of alerts that are real
  • Mean time to acknowledge - How fast alerts are picked up
  • Mean time to resolve - Time from alert to closure
  • Coverage - % of assets monitored

Signs You're Ready for Level 3

  • Comprehensive log coverage across environment
  • True positive rate above 50%
  • Documented playbooks for all common alert types
  • Defined escalation paths
  • Basic metrics tracked and reported
