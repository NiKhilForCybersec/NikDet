🎯 Real Talk About Salaries

These ranges assume US market. To maximize compensation:

  • Location: SF, NYC, Seattle pay most (but cost of living!)
  • Company type: Big tech > Finance > Enterprise > MSSP
  • Job hopping: Switching jobs every 2-3 years often beats staying
  • Negotiation: Always negotiate. Research market rates first.

🔍 Detection Engineering

What you do: Build, tune, and maintain detection rules. Make the SIEM actually catch bad things.

Skills needed: Query languages (KQL, SPL), MITRE ATT&CK, understanding of attacks, data analysis

Day-to-day: Writing rules, reducing false positives, coverage gap analysis, working with threat intel

Typical titles: Detection Engineer, Security Content Developer, SIEM Engineer

🎯 Threat Hunting

What you do: Proactively search for threats that evaded detection. Find attackers before they succeed.

Skills needed: Advanced query skills, attack techniques, hypothesis development, data analysis

Day-to-day: Developing hunt hypotheses, running queries, analyzing anomalies, creating new detections

Typical titles: Threat Hunter, Senior Security Analyst (Hunting), Cyber Threat Hunter

🚨 Incident Response

What you do: Lead response to security incidents. Contain, eradicate, recover from attacks.

Skills needed: Forensics, crisis management, communication, technical breadth

Day-to-day: Leading IR calls, coordinating response, forensic analysis, writing reports

Typical titles: Incident Response Lead, IR Manager, CSIRT Analyst

🛠️ Security Engineering

What you do: Build and maintain security infrastructure. Automation, tooling, architecture.

Skills needed: Programming (Python, Go), cloud (AWS/Azure/GCP), infrastructure, DevOps

Day-to-day: Building tools, automation, infrastructure design, security architecture

Typical titles: Security Engineer, Security Automation Engineer, SecOps Engineer

🔴 Red Team / Penetration Testing

What you do: Attack your own organization to find weaknesses before real attackers do.

Skills needed: Offensive techniques, programming, networking, creativity

Day-to-day: Planning engagements, conducting attacks, writing reports, recommending fixes

Typical titles: Red Team Operator, Penetration Tester, Offensive Security Engineer

How to Actually Advance

ADVANCEMENT STRATEGIES THAT WORK ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════ STRATEGY 1: VISIBILITY ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── Get your work SEEN: • Volunteer for high-visibility projects • Present findings to leadership • Write internal blog posts / documentation • Share wins in team meetings "The analyst who catches a major attack AND communicates it well gets promoted faster than the one who just catches it." STRATEGY 2: BUILD A PORTFOLIO ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── Document your accomplishments: • Major incidents you handled • Detections you built • Process improvements • Automation scripts • Presentations given At promotion/interview time, you have PROOF of your value. STRATEGY 3: CERTIFICATIONS (Strategic) ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── Not all certs are equal. Focus on: Early career: Security+, CySA+, BTL1 Mid career: GCIH, GCIA, GCFA, AZ-500 Advanced: OSCP, GREM, SANS 500+ series Cert + experience > Cert alone Experience > Cert without experience STRATEGY 4: NETWORK ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── Your network = your opportunities: • Attend local meetups (BSides, OWASP) • Engage on Twitter/LinkedIn • Join Discord communities • Help others (it comes back) STRATEGY 5: KNOW WHEN TO MOVE ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── Sometimes the path forward is OUT: Signs it's time to leave: • No growth opportunities • Underpaid for market • Not learning anymore • Toxic environment • Better role elsewhere Average tenure before job hop: 2-3 years

💡 The Underrated Career Move: Teach Others

Want to solidify your knowledge AND get noticed? Teach.

  • Internal: Train new analysts, create documentation
  • External: Blog posts, conference talks, YouTube tutorials

Teaching forces you to truly understand topics, builds your reputation, and helps the community. Many senior roles are filled by people known in the community.

Career Mistakes to Avoid

COMMON CAREER MISTAKES ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════ ❌ MISTAKE: Collecting certifications without experience WHY BAD: Certs show you studied, not that you can DO FIX: Get hands-on experience alongside certs ❌ MISTAKE: Staying at one company forever WHY BAD: Often leaves money on the table, limits exposure FIX: Evaluate market every 2-3 years. Staying should be a choice. ❌ MISTAKE: Only doing what's assigned WHY BAD: You blend in with everyone else FIX: Volunteer for projects, improve processes, go beyond ❌ MISTAKE: Neglecting soft skills WHY BAD: Technical skills plateau; soft skills differentiate FIX: Practice communication, documentation, presenting ❌ MISTAKE: Waiting for promotion to come to you WHY BAD: Proactive people get promoted FIX: Tell your manager your goals, ask what you need to do ❌ MISTAKE: Specializing too early WHY BAD: You don't know what you like yet FIX: Build broad foundation (L1-L2), then specialize
