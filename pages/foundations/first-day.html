Your First Day as a SOC Analyst

Nervous? Everyone is on day one. This guide tells you exactly what to expect, what to do, and how to succeed in your first weeks. You've got this.

🌟 First: Take a Deep Breath

Everyone started where you are. That senior analyst who seems to know everything? They were once sitting where you're sitting, feeling exactly what you're feeling. The difference between you and them is just time and experience - both of which you're about to get.

Questions to Ask (And When)

QUESTIONS FOR DAY 1 ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════ ASK YOUR MANAGER: ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── • "What does success look like in my first 30/60/90 days?" • "Who should I go to with questions?" • "What's the most important thing for me to learn first?" • "How often will we have 1:1s?" • "What's the team's biggest challenge right now?" ASK YOUR TEAMMATES: ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── • "What do you wish you knew when you started?" • "What's the most common mistake new analysts make?" • "What's your favorite resource for learning?" • "What alerts do you see most often?" • "Can I shadow you sometime?" ASK ABOUT TOOLS: ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── • "Which tools will I use most?" • "Is there training material for [SIEM/EDR/etc]?" • "What's the process for getting access to [system]?" • "Are there any sandbox/training environments I can practice in?" DON'T BE AFRAID TO ASK: ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── • "Can you explain that again?" • "Where can I find documentation on this?" • "Is this normal or should I escalate?" • "I'm stuck - can you help me think through this?" • "How do I know when to escalate?"

💡 Pro Tip: The Question Strategy

Before asking, try:

  1. Search the wiki/documentation (30 seconds)
  2. Search Slack/chat history (30 seconds)
  3. Google it (30 seconds)

Then ask: "I looked in [place] but couldn't find [thing]. Can you point me in the right direction?"

This shows initiative while still getting help. Senior analysts appreciate when you've tried first.

🌟 Remember

Your first alerts will take 20-30 minutes. That's okay. Experienced analysts take 5 minutes. You'll get there. Speed comes with pattern recognition, and pattern recognition comes with practice.

Common First-Week Mistakes (And How to Avoid Them)

❌ Mistake 1: Pretending to Understand

What happens: Senior analyst explains something. You nod even though you're lost. Later, you're stuck and embarrassed to ask.

Better approach: "I want to make sure I understand - can you explain [part] again?" Nobody expects you to get everything first time.

❌ Mistake 2: Not Taking Notes

What happens: Someone shows you how to do something. Next day, you've forgotten.

Better approach: Always have a notebook (physical or digital). Write down steps, commands, URLs. Your notes become your personal playbook.

❌ Mistake 3: Closing Alerts Too Quickly

What happens: You want to show you're productive, so you close alerts fast without proper investigation.

Better approach: Quality over quantity. One well-investigated alert is worth more than ten rushed closures. You're building habits now.

❌ Mistake 4: Not Asking for Help When Stuck

What happens: You spend 2 hours on something an experienced analyst could solve in 5 minutes.

Better approach: Time-box yourself. "I'll try for 15 minutes, then ask for help." This is efficient, not weak.

❌ Mistake 5: Comparing Yourself to Senior Analysts

What happens: You feel dumb because others know so much more.

Better approach: They have years of experience. Compare yourself to where you were yesterday, not where they are today.

Your 30/60/90 Day Plan

📅 Days 1-30: Learn the Basics

Focus: Understanding tools, processes, and your environment

  • Complete all onboarding and training
  • Shadow experienced analysts
  • Handle alerts with supervision
  • Learn the top 10 alert types you'll see
  • Memorize the escalation process
  • Read all playbooks at least once

Success metric: You can handle common alerts independently

📅 Days 31-60: Build Confidence

Focus: Independence and efficiency

  • Handle alerts independently (escalate when needed)
  • Reduce time-per-alert
  • Identify patterns in false positives
  • Submit your first tuning request
  • Contribute in team meetings
  • Start learning advanced topics

Success metric: You're contributing meaningfully to the team

📅 Days 61-90: Add Value

Focus: Contributing beyond basic triage

  • Handle escalations confidently
  • Mentor newer team members
  • Improve a playbook or process
  • Present an interesting case in team meeting
  • Identify a detection gap and propose solution
  • Complete a certification or training

Success metric: The team is better because you're on it
