What RevFlow Sells

Subscription software platform used by enterprise sales organizations for deal prediction, forecasting, and sales intelligence. It does not sell data — it sells insight from the customer's own data.

Who The Customers Are

Large enterprises with complex sales organizations: Technology Giants (Cisco, Dell, HP, Oracle), Financial Institutions, Global B2B SaaS companies, Industrial Enterprises. Each customer typically has thousands of sales users and strict compliance requirements.

Why This Migration Happened

The Origin Story (2015-2022)

RevFlow was founded in 2015 as an enterprise analytics startup. Like many B2B SaaS companies of that era, they chose on-premises hosted infrastructure:

  • Co-located Data Center: Predictable costs, enterprise customers trusted "dedicated infrastructure"
  • VMware Virtualization: Industry standard, team expertise
  • On-Prem Active Directory: Required for Windows servers, GPO management
  • Traditional Security Stack: RSA NetWitness (SIEM), ESET (endpoints), Symantec (DLP)

🎯 Key Interview Point

"The product is SaaS, but the infrastructure was on-prem." RevFlow delivered a SaaS product to customers, but internally hosted it on their own VMware infrastructure. This is common for enterprise SaaS companies started pre-2018.

The Siemens Acquisition (2022)

Siemens acquisition triggered several changes:

  • Azure-First Cloud Strategy: Siemens subsidiaries aligned on cloud-first strategy
  • Microsoft 365 E5 Standardization: Includes MDE, MDI, MDO, Sentinel, Purview, MDCA
  • Data Center Exit Timeline: Co-location lease renewal coming in 2025

Interview-Ready Answers

🎯 "Tell me about the company you worked for"

"It's a Siemens subsidiary — a B2B SaaS analytics platform that helps enterprise sales teams predict deal outcomes. Customers connect their Salesforce CRM, and the platform analyzes patterns from historical deals to predict which current deals are likely to close and which are at risk.

The customers are large enterprises — companies like Cisco, Dell, major financial institutions. Each customer has thousands of sales users and strict security requirements. They're trusting us with their CRM data.

From a security perspective, this shaped our priorities significantly. We're a data processor, not owner — so tenant isolation, API security, and data protection were more important than traditional perimeter controls."

🎯 "How did the business model affect your security approach?"

"Completely. As a B2B SaaS processing customer data via API integrations, our security priorities were different from a traditional enterprise.

First, tenant isolation — we needed to ensure no cross-customer data leakage. Every query, every log, every detection rule had to be tenant-aware.

Second, API security — we're pulling data from customer Salesforce instances via OAuth. Protecting those credentials, monitoring for abuse, rate limiting — that's critical.

Third, SaaS visibility — our detection strategy prioritized Salesforce, Zoom, and Teams audit logs over traditional network perimeter monitoring. The crown jewels aren't in the datacenter; they're in SaaS applications.

And finally, compliance as a business requirement — Fortune 500 customers won't even consider you without SOC 2 Type II. Security isn't just risk management; it's a sales enabler."

🎯 "Why legacy tools at a SaaS company?"

"The product is SaaS, but the infrastructure was on-premises. RevFlow was founded in 2015 and built on traditional data center infrastructure — VMware, on-prem Active Directory, co-located servers. The security stack — RSA, ESET, Symantec — made sense for that architecture.

After the Siemens acquisition in 2022, leadership adopted a cloud-first approach. The data center lease is expiring, we already have M365 E5 with security tools included, and maintaining three separate on-prem security platforms was consuming too much of the team's time. The security migration is part of the larger Azure transformation."
