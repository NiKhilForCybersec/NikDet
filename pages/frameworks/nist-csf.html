NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0

A voluntary framework of standards, guidelines, and best practices for managing cybersecurity risk.

What is NIST CSF?

The NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) provides a common language for organizations to understand, manage, and communicate cybersecurity risk. Version 2.0 (released 2024) adds a new "Govern" function and expands scope beyond critical infrastructure.

Key Facts
  • Publisher: National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)
  • Current Version: CSF 2.0 (February 2024)
  • Scope: All organizations, all sectors
  • Cost: Free to use

The 6 Functions

CSF 2.0 organizes cybersecurity activities into six high-level functions:

GOVERN

Strategy & Oversight

IDENTIFY

Asset & Risk Management

PROTECT

Safeguards

DETECT

Anomaly Detection

RESPOND

Incident Response

RECOVER

Restoration

Function Details

GOVERN (GV) - NEW in 2.0

Establish and monitor the organization's cybersecurity risk management strategy, expectations, and policy.

CategoryDescription
Organizational Context (GV.OC)Understand mission, stakeholders, requirements
Risk Management Strategy (GV.RM)Establish risk priorities and tolerance
Roles & Responsibilities (GV.RR)Establish accountability for cybersecurity
Policy (GV.PO)Organizational cybersecurity policy
Oversight (GV.OV)Review and adjust strategy
Supply Chain (GV.SC)Manage supply chain risk

IDENTIFY (ID)

Understand organizational context, assets, and cybersecurity risks.

CategoryDescriptionSOC Relevance
Asset Management (ID.AM)Inventory and manage assetsKnow what to protect
Risk Assessment (ID.RA)Identify and prioritize risksFocus detection efforts
Improvement (ID.IM)Continuous improvement processesLearn from incidents

PROTECT (PR)

Implement safeguards to ensure delivery of critical services.

CategoryDescriptionSOC Relevance

Data Security (PR.DS)Protect data at rest/transitDLP monitoring

DETECT (DE)

Discover cybersecurity events in a timely manner. This is core SOC territory.

CategoryDescriptionSOC Activities
Continuous Monitoring (DE.CM)Monitor for anomaliesSIEM alerts, EDR monitoring, threat hunting
Adverse Event Analysis (DE.AE)Analyze detected eventsAlert triage, investigation, correlation

RESPOND (RS)

Take action regarding detected cybersecurity incidents.

CategoryDescriptionSOC Activities
Incident Management (RS.MA)Manage incidentsIR playbooks, containment, eradication
Incident Analysis (RS.AN)Analyze incidentsRoot cause analysis, forensics
Incident Response (RS.RP)Execute responseContainment actions, remediation
Incident Mitigation (RS.MI)Prevent expansionIsolation, blocking, patching

RECOVER (RC)

Restore capabilities impaired by cybersecurity incidents.

CategoryDescription
Incident Recovery Plan (RC.RP)Execute recovery activities
Incident Recovery Communication (RC.CO)Coordinate restoration efforts

Implementation Tiers

CSF defines four tiers describing the rigor of risk management practices:

TierNameCharacteristics
1PartialAd hoc, reactive, limited awareness
2Risk InformedSome processes, not organization-wide
3RepeatableFormal policies, regularly updated
4AdaptiveContinuous improvement, predictive

Profiles

A profile aligns the framework functions with your business requirements, risk tolerance, and resources:

  • Current Profile: Where you are today
  • Target Profile: Where you want to be
  • Gap Analysis: Difference between current and target
