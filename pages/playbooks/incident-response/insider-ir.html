PB-IR-005

Insider Threat Incident Response

Response procedures for suspected malicious or negligent insider activity. Requires close coordination with HR and Legal.

Legal and HR Coordination Required

Insider threat investigations have significant legal and employment implications. Always coordinate with HR and Legal before taking action. Document everything assuming it may be used in court.

Insider Threat Categories

CategoryIntentExamplesResponse Approach
MaliciousIntentional harmData theft, sabotage, fraudLegal/HR first, covert investigation
NegligentUnintentionalSending data to wrong person, poor security practicesEducation, policy enforcement
CompromisedVictim of external attackPhished credentials, coercedTreat as external incident initially
Departing EmployeeVariesTaking data before leavingProactive monitoring, exit procedures

Key Stakeholders

StakeholderRoleWhen to Engage

LegalLegal guidance, litigation hold, evidenceAlways - for anything beyond negligence

Critical Rules

  • ❌ Never confront the employee directly - HR handles all communication
  • ❌ Never discuss with colleagues - Strict need-to-know only
  • ❌ Never take punitive action without HR/Legal - Could create liability
  • ✅ Document everything - Assume court review
  • ✅ Preserve all evidence - Follow chain of custody
  • ✅ Be objective - Facts only, no speculation

Response by Scenario

ScenarioUrgencyFirst Steps
Active data theft in progressCriticalCovertly contain (revoke access if approved by HR/Legal), preserve evidence
Discovered historical theftHighEngage HR/Legal, assess scope, preserve evidence before any action
Negligent data exposureMediumContain the exposure, engage HR for policy discussion
Departing employee data accessHighMonitor, restrict access, coordinate exit with HR
Disgruntled employee concernsMediumEnhanced monitoring, HR engagement, document concerns

Investigation Focus Areas

  • What data was accessed? - File access logs, DLP alerts, email attachments
  • Where did it go? - Personal email, cloud storage, USB, print
  • When did it happen? - Timeline of activity, before/after key dates
  • Is there a pattern? - Ongoing behavior vs. one-time event
  • What's the motive? - Departing? Competitor? Personal use?

Investigation Queries

// User file activity over time
OfficeActivity
| where TimeGenerated > ago(90d)
| where UserId == "suspect@company.com"
| where Operation in ("FileDownloaded", "FileAccessed", "FileSyncDownloadedFull")
| summarize DownloadCount = count() by bin(TimeGenerated, 1d)
| render timechart

// Emails to personal domains
EmailEvents
| where TimeGenerated > ago(30d)
| where SenderFromAddress == "suspect@company.com"
| where RecipientEmailAddress endswith "gmail.com" or RecipientEmailAddress endswith "yahoo.com"
| project TimeGenerated, RecipientEmailAddress, Subject, AttachmentCount

// USB/Removable device usage
DeviceEvents
| where TimeGenerated > ago(30d)
| where DeviceName == "SUSPECT-PC"
| where ActionType == "UsbDriveMounted"
| project TimeGenerated, DeviceName, AdditionalFields

Documentation Requirements

Every action must be documented assuming legal review:

  • Date and time of all findings and actions
  • Who made the finding or took the action
  • What evidence was collected and where it's stored
  • Chain of custody for all evidence
  • All communications with HR and Legal
  • Rationale for all decisions
