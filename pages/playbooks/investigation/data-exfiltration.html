Data Exfiltration Investigation

Investigating potential unauthorized data transfer out of the organization.

Why This Investigation Matters

Data Exfiltration = The Actual Damage

Everything attackers do leads to this. Data exfiltration is often the final goal - whether for:

TypeData TargetedBusiness Impact
Financial theftBanking credentials, payment dataDirect monetary loss
IP theftSource code, designs, formulasCompetitive advantage lost, billions possible
Ransom leverageAny sensitive dataDouble extortion - pay or data published
EspionageStrategic plans, communicationsNational security, market position
PII theftCustomer/employee personal dataRegulatory fines, lawsuits, reputation

How to Investigate

Step 1: Identify What Data Was Transferred

// KQL - Files accessed before large transfer
DeviceFileEvents
| where Timestamp between (datetime("transfer-time-minus-1h") .. datetime("transfer-time"))
| where DeviceName == "USER-DEVICE"
| where ActionType in ("FileRead", "FileCopied")
| project Timestamp, FileName, FolderPath, FileSize
| order by FileSize desc

// KQL - Archive creation activity
DeviceProcessEvents
| where Timestamp > ago(24h)
| where DeviceName == "USER-DEVICE"
| where FileName in~ ("7z.exe", "winrar.exe", "zip.exe", "tar.exe") 
    or ProcessCommandLine has_any (".zip", ".rar", ".7z", "Compress-Archive")
| project Timestamp, ProcessCommandLine, AccountName

// KQL - Cloud upload activity
CloudAppEvents
| where Timestamp > ago(24h)
| where AccountId == "user@company.com"
| where ActionType in ("FileUploaded", "FileShared")
| project Timestamp, Application, ActionType, ObjectName, ObjectType
| order by Timestamp desc

What If Scenarios

What If...Then...Rationale
Data was encrypted before transfer?Can't determine content. Assume worst case based on source location. Interview user.Encryption is deliberate evasion - treat as high severity
Destination IP is untraceable (TOR)?This is confirmed malicious. No legitimate business use for TOR exfil.TOR = intentional anonymization
User claims they "always do this"?Verify with logs. If true, it's a policy problem. If false, it's a lie about theft.Historical pattern is verifiable
This is a departing employee?High probability of intentional theft. Preserve all evidence. Legal/HR involvement.Departing employees are highest insider threat risk
Data already left and we can't recover it?Focus shifts to: What was taken? Who needs to be notified? How do we prevent next time?Can't un-ring the bell, but can manage aftermath
This is an attacker, not insider?This is end-stage of compromise. Find entry point, identify all stolen data, full IR engagement.Attacker exfil means significant prior compromise

Containment Actions

Immediate Actions

ScenarioActionHow
Active exfil in progressBlock immediatelyIsolate endpoint, block destination IP/domain, disable account
Departing employee suspectedPreserve evidence, legal holdImage device, preserve cloud data, don't alert employee yet
Compromised account exfilStop attacker accessDisable account, revoke sessions, isolate systems
Exfil completed (historical)Evidence preservationForensic image, log preservation, impact assessment

Do NOT Do

  • Don't confront employee without HR/Legal - Could compromise investigation or create liability
  • Don't let employee know they're being investigated - They may destroy evidence
  • Don't assume only one transfer occurred - Look for pattern of behavior
  • Don't forget personal devices - May have synced/transferred data there too

Escalation Criteria

Escalate Immediately When:
  • PII/PHI/regulated data confirmed exfiltrated
  • Source code or trade secrets transferred
  • Executive data accessed/transferred
  • Destination is known competitor
  • Volume exceeds 1GB
  • Evidence of deliberate evasion (encryption, DNS tunnel)
  • Attacker-controlled exfiltration (not insider)
