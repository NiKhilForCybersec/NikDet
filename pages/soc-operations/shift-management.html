SOC Shift Management

Complete guide to managing 24/7 SOC operations, shift handovers, staffing, and team effectiveness.

Part 1: Common Shift Models

24/7 Coverage Models

ModelDescriptionProsCons
Follow-the-Sun Handoff between global locations (US → EU → APAC) No night shifts, better work-life balance Requires multiple locations, complex handovers
4x10 Hour Four 10-hour shifts per week 3 days off, fewer handovers Long days, fatigue at end of shift
3x12 Hour Three 12-hour shifts, then 3-4 days off Extended time off, fewer handovers Very long shifts, burnout risk
5x8 Hour (Rotating) Standard 8-hour shifts, rotating day/night Standard hours, manageable Sleep disruption from rotation
Panama Schedule 2 days on, 2 off, 3 on, 2 off, 2 on, 3 off Every other weekend off, predictable Complex schedule, some long stretches

Part 2: Staffing Requirements

Minimum Staffing Calculation

Formula: Analysts Needed = (Hours to Cover × Days per Week) ÷ (Hours per Analyst per Week)

Example: 24 hours × 7 days = 168 hours ÷ 40 hours = 4.2 analysts minimum

Add 20-30% for PTO, sick days, training → Need 5-6 analysts for true 24/7 coverage

Staffing by SOC Size

SOC SizeCoverageMinimum StaffTypical Structure
Small Business hours + on-call 2-4 analysts L1/L2 combined, L3 outsourced
Medium Extended hours (16h) + on-call 6-10 analysts L1 and L2 tiers, shared L3
Large 24/7 coverage 15-25 analysts Full L1/L2/L3 tiers, shift leads
Enterprise Follow-the-sun 24/7 30+ analysts Multiple global SOCs, specialized teams

Part 3: Shift Handover Process

The handover is the most critical moment in shift operations. Poor handovers lead to dropped incidents, repeated work, and gaps in coverage.

Handover Checklist

1
Active Incidents (15 min before shift end)

Review all open/in-progress incidents. Document current status, next steps, and any blockers.

2
Queue Status

How many alerts in queue? Any aged alerts that need attention? Priority items?

3
Environment Issues

Any known SIEM issues? Log sources down? Planned maintenance? Tool outages?

4
Threat Intel Updates

New threat campaigns? IOCs to watch for? Active exploitation of new vulnerabilities?

5
Verbal Handover (5-10 min)

Face-to-face or video call to discuss critical items. Allow questions.

Daily Standup Format

Brief sync meeting (10-15 minutes max) covering:

  • What happened since last standup? Major incidents, notable events
  • What's the current queue status? Backlog, blockers
  • Any help needed? Escalations, collaboration requests
  • Threat intel updates? New campaigns, IOCs

Part 6: Burnout Prevention

SOC analysts face high stress, alert fatigue, and irregular hours. Managing burnout is essential for retention and effectiveness.

Warning Signs of Burnout

  • Decreased alert quality (missing obvious items)
  • Increased absenteeism
  • Cynicism about the job or security in general
  • Decreased participation in team activities
  • Reluctance to take on new challenges

Prevention Strategies

StrategyImplementation
Rotation Rotate between L1 triage and project work (detection engineering, hunting)
Training Time Dedicate 10-20% of time to learning and development
Alert Tuning Continuously reduce false positives to decrease noise
Automation Automate repetitive tasks (enrichment, common responses)
Fair Scheduling Equitable distribution of nights, weekends, holidays
Recognition Acknowledge good catches, celebrate wins
