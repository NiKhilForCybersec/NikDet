Understanding the SOC: its purpose, structure, functions, and why it matters to the organization.

Why Does a SOC Exist?

The Core Problem

The Fundamental Challenge

Common Questions About SOC

Why is the SOC 24/7? Can't we just check in the morning?

Attackers don't work business hours. In fact, they often attack at night and weekends when they expect less monitoring. Ransomware is frequently deployed Friday night for maximum impact. Without 24/7 coverage, attacks can progress for 16+ hours before anyone notices.

Why do we have so many false positives?

Detection is hard. The difference between attack and normal activity is often subtle. Detections must be sensitive enough to catch real threats, which means some legitimate activity looks suspicious. This is why we have analysts - to make the judgment call that automation can't.

Good SOCs continuously tune detections to reduce FPs while maintaining detection capability.

Can't AI/ML replace SOC analysts?

Not yet, and maybe never completely. AI/ML is great at pattern matching and anomaly detection, but attacks are creative and context-dependent. A skilled attacker can evade ML models. Human judgment is still required for:

  • Understanding business context
  • Making containment decisions with business impact
  • Communicating with stakeholders
  • Handling novel attacks

AI augments analysts by handling volume and highlighting anomalies, but doesn't replace them.

What's the difference between SOC and CSIRT/IR team?
SOCContinuous monitoring, triage, initial response. Handles day-to-day security events.
CSIRT/IRHandles major incidents. May be activated by SOC for significant events. More forensics, recovery, lessons learned focus.

In smaller organizations, these may be the same team. In larger orgs, they're often separate but work closely together.
