Ransomware Defense Model

Complete kill-chain detection strategy with 25+ detection rules, controls by plane, and metrics.

Why This Matters

Ransomware is not detected by a single rule. It's a multi-stage attack with many opportunities for detection. A mature SOC detects ransomware at multiple points in the kill chain — if you only detect the final encryption, you've already lost.

The Detection Window

Ransomware operators typically spend 5-21 days in an environment before encrypting. This is your detection window. Every stage is a detection opportunity.

Goal: Detect at Stage 1-4 (before credential theft) = minimal damage. Detect at Stage 5-8 = containable. Detect at Stage 9-11 = significant damage.

Interview Answer

"Ransomware defense requires a kill-chain approach—you can't rely on a single detection rule. I map the attack stages using MITRE ATT&CK, from initial access through impact, and ensure we have detection coverage at each stage. That typically means 20-25+ detection rules covering phishing, suspicious execution, persistence mechanisms, credential dumping, lateral movement, and finally encryption behaviors.

But detection isn't enough. I layer preventive controls across all three planes: Data plane controls like EDR in block mode and ASR rules; Control plane controls like MFA, tiered admin model, and Credential Guard; and Management plane controls to protect GPO, backup systems, and security tools from tampering.

I align everything to NIST CSF—Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover—to ensure completeness.

Finally, I track metrics at each level: detection coverage percentage, MTTD under 24 hours, MTTR under 4 hours, and regular testing through purple team exercises and backup restoration tests. The goal is to detect early in the kill chain—ideally before credential theft—and have proven recovery capability if everything else fails."
