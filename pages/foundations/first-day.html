Your First Day as a SOC Analyst

Nervous about your first day? This guide walks you through exactly what to expect, what to ask, and how to succeed in your first weeks as a SOC analyst.

You've Got This

Everyone was new once. Your team expects you to learn, not to know everything. Ask questions - that's how you grow.

Before Your First Day

Things to Review

  • Basic networking concepts (TCP/IP, ports, DNS)
  • Windows event log basics
  • What a SIEM is and does
  • Common attack types (phishing, malware, brute force)
  • The company's industry and what they protect

Things NOT to Worry About

  • Knowing the company's specific tools - you'll be trained
  • Memorizing every attack technique - that comes with experience
  • Being as fast as senior analysts - speed comes with time
  • Making mistakes - everyone makes them, especially early on

Your First Alert - Step by Step

Your first real alert will feel overwhelming. Here's exactly what to do:

1
Don't Panic

Take a breath. Read the alert completely. Understand what it's telling you before doing anything.

2
Check for a Playbook

Ask: "Is there a documented procedure for this alert type?" Follow it step by step.

3
Gather Basic Context

Who? (user/system), What? (the activity), When? (timestamp), Where? (source IP/location)

4
Check Historical Alerts

Has this user/system generated this alert before? Was it a false positive?

5
Ask for Help if Unsure

It's always better to ask than to close something you shouldn't or miss something important.

6
Document Everything

Write down what you found, what you did, and why. Even if it's a false positive.

Common First-Week Fears (And Why They're OK)

FearReality
"I don't understand this alert"Normal. Ask someone to explain it. That's learning.
"Everyone seems so much faster than me"They've been doing this for years. You'll get there.
"What if I miss something important?"You're shadowing and learning. Senior analysts review your work.
"I don't know the tools"Tool proficiency comes with daily use. Focus on concepts first.
"I asked a dumb question"There are no dumb questions when you're learning. Better to ask than assume.
"I made a mistake"Everyone does. Own it, learn from it, move on.

First Week Survival Tips

  • Take lots of notes: You'll forget things. Write them down.
  • Set up a cheat sheet: URLs to tools, login procedures, escalation contacts
  • Shadow actively: Ask "why" at each step, not just "what"
  • Don't compare yourself to seniors: They have years of experience
  • Learn the environment: What systems exist, what's normal here
  • Understand the business: What does the company do? What's most critical?
  • Sleep well: Alert brain needs rest

Building Good Habits Early

  • Always document: Even if you think it's obvious
  • Verify before closing: Double-check your conclusion
  • Ask before acting: Especially for response actions
  • Stay curious: Learn something new every day
  • Help others: Once you learn something, teach the next new person

Red Flags in Your New Job

Most SOCs are great places to learn. But watch out for:

  • No training or onboarding plan
  • Punished for asking questions
  • No escalation path when stuck
  • Excessive overtime without support
  • No playbooks or documentation
  • Toxic team environment
