MITRE ATT&CK Framework

Understanding and applying the MITRE ATT&CK framework for detection, hunting, and security operations.

Why MITRE ATT&CK Matters

ATT&CK is Your Common Language

MITRE ATT&CK provides a standardized way to describe attacker behavior. Instead of vague descriptions like "advanced malware," you can say "T1059.001 PowerShell execution leading to T1003.001 LSASS memory credential dumping."

How SOC Teams Use ATT&CK

Use CaseHow ATT&CK HelpsExample
Detection engineeringOrganize detections by technique"We have 15 detections covering T1059 (Command and Scripting)"
Coverage analysisIdentify gaps in detection"We have no coverage for T1550 (Use Alternate Authentication)"
Threat intelMap threat actors to techniques"APT29 uses T1078 (Valid Accounts) - do we detect this?"
Incident reportingStandardized technique references"Attacker used T1566.001 (Spearphishing Attachment) for initial access"
HuntingSystematic hypothesis generation"Let's hunt for T1053.005 (Scheduled Task persistence)"
CommunicationCommon language with other teams/vendors"Our EDR detects these ATT&CK techniques..."

The Hierarchy

LevelWhat It IsExample

Common ATT&CK Mistakes

MistakeWhy It's a ProblemBetter Approach
Trying to detect everything Impossible, leads to burnout and alert fatigue Prioritize based on threat intel and risk
One detection per technique Techniques have many variations Multiple detections per technique, cover variations
Checking boxes without testing Paper coverage isn't real coverage Validate detections with red team/purple team
Ignoring sub-techniques Missing specific implementation details Use sub-techniques for precise detection
Not updating coverage ATT&CK evolves, so do attackers Regular coverage reviews, quarterly updates
