Lateral Movement Investigation

Investigating unauthorized movement between systems - a critical indicator of active intrusion.

Why This Investigation Matters

Lateral Movement = Active Attacker

Lateral movement means an attacker has moved past initial access and is actively exploring your network. This is one of the most critical alerts you can receive.

What It MeansWhy It's Serious
Attacker has valid credentialsInitial compromise already succeeded
They're looking for valuable targetsData, DCs, critical systems at risk
They can expand access quicklyMinutes to hours from one system to many
They may already have what they needRansomware or exfil may be imminent

How to Investigate

Step 1: Identify the Source (Patient Zero)

// KQL - Track lateral movement source
DeviceLogonEvents
| where Timestamp > ago(24h)
| where LogonType in ("RemoteInteractive", "Network", "NetworkCleartext")
| where DeviceName == "TARGET-SYSTEM"
| project Timestamp, AccountName, AccountDomain, LogonType, 
    RemoteIP, RemoteDeviceName, Protocol
| order by Timestamp asc

// KQL - PsExec/service installation tracking
DeviceProcessEvents
| where Timestamp > ago(24h)
| where FileName in~ ("psexec.exe", "psexesvc.exe") 
    or ProcessCommandLine has_any ("psexec", "-s cmd", "/s cmd")
| project Timestamp, DeviceName, FileName, ProcessCommandLine, 
    AccountName, InitiatingProcessFileName

// KQL - Remote WMI execution
DeviceProcessEvents
| where Timestamp > ago(24h)
| where InitiatingProcessFileName =~ "WmiPrvSE.exe"
| where FileName != "WmiPrvSE.exe"
| project Timestamp, DeviceName, FileName, ProcessCommandLine, AccountName

Step 2: Map the Movement Path

// KQL - All systems touched by suspicious account
let suspiciousAccount = "compromised-user";
DeviceLogonEvents
| where Timestamp > ago(7d)
| where AccountName =~ suspiciousAccount
| where LogonType in ("RemoteInteractive", "Network")
| summarize 
    FirstSeen = min(Timestamp),
    LastSeen = max(Timestamp),
    LogonCount = count()
    by DeviceName, LogonType
| order by FirstSeen asc

// KQL - Network connections from compromised system
DeviceNetworkEvents
| where Timestamp > ago(24h)
| where DeviceName == "COMPROMISED-SYSTEM"
| where RemotePort in (445, 135, 3389, 5985, 22, 23)
| summarize Connections = count() by RemoteIP, RemotePort
| order by Connections desc

// KQL - Visual timeline of movement
DeviceLogonEvents
| where Timestamp > ago(24h)
| where AccountName =~ "compromised-user"
| where ActionType == "LogonSuccess"
| project Timestamp, 
    Movement = strcat(RemoteDeviceName, " → ", DeviceName),
    LogonType, Protocol
| order by Timestamp asc

What If Scenarios

What If...Then...Rationale
Lateral movement is from IT admin during work hours?Still verify. Call them, check ticket system. If legitimate, document and tune detection.Attackers steal IT credentials specifically to blend in
They already reached domain controller?Assume full domain compromise. Start planning AD recovery, KRBTGT reset, all credential reset.DC access = attacker can create any credential
I can see them moving but can't identify source?Focus on blocking at known points. Segment network. They may be using covert channels.Some advanced attackers use tunneling to hide source
Movement stopped before I could investigate?They may have achieved their goal. Check for exfil, persistence, any impact. Don't assume it's over.Attackers stage then execute; quiet period may precede impact
Multiple accounts are doing lateral movement?Large-scale compromise. Multiple credentials stolen. Escalate immediately, consider network isolation.Multiple accounts = advanced attacker with broad access

Escalation Criteria

Immediate Escalation Required When:
  • Domain controller accessed
  • Pass-the-Hash or Pass-the-Ticket detected
  • More than 3 systems compromised
  • Domain Admin credentials used
  • Unable to identify source of movement
  • Critical systems (finance, HR, exec) accessed
  • Evidence of credential dumping (Mimikatz, LSASS access)
