Foundation Questions

Why Consulting?

In-house becomes repetitive once processes stabilize — same tickets daily. I enjoy the "figure it out" phase — assessments, roadmaps, solving new problems. Want variety: different clients, industries, challenges. I'll learn faster seeing 10 different security programs than staying in one for 10 years.

Greatest Strength

Communication: Adjust explanations for different audiences (VP vs. colleague). Root cause focus: Don't just patch — understand why, then document it. Documentation habit saves time for whole team. "Fix it, understand why, write it down."

Greatest Weakness

Early career: preferred working alone, hesitated to ask for help. Learned: Nobody judges — they appreciate faster problem resolution. Now: If stuck 30-60 min, reach out immediately. "Here's what I tried, what am I missing?"

AI Security (Hot Topic)

Your Position on AI Risks

"My take: AI is extremely powerful, but the real risk isn't that AI suddenly creates elite attacks — it's that it accelerates ideation and lowers the barrier to misuse. Models lack execution feedback, environmental context, and iterative tuning. Existing controls (EDR, email security, identity) still work. AI security is a governance and detection problem as much as a technology problem."

AI Limitations

Economics is NOT the bottleneck — Microsoft $13B+, Google $30B+ annual spend. Physics IS the limiter: speed of light, heat dissipation, atomic scale. Time-to-build: chip fabs 3-5 years, power grid 5-10 years. Memory wall: processors fast but memory sits far — Nvidia's 3D stacking (HBM3e) helps with 30x inference gains.

Prompt Injection

Direct: "Ignore previous instructions..." Indirect: Malicious content in docs/websites (resume hidden text trick). Risk: Data exfil, unauthorized actions. No perfect fix — defense in depth: input validation, output filtering, privilege separation, human-in-the-loop. "Treat LLM output as untrusted."

SIEM Recommendations

Which SIEM Do You Recommend?

"It depends on the client's ecosystem." Microsoft shop + cloud-first → Sentinel. Multi-cloud + mature SOC → Splunk. Cost-sensitive + simpler needs → Elastic, Chronicle. Key factors: Existing investments, team skills, log volume, compliance needs. Never say "X is always best."

XDR vs SIEM — The Plane Model

Data Plane (what's happening) → XDR handles: logins, file access, process execution. Management Plane (who changed configs) → SIEM required: admin actions, policy changes, privilege modifications. "XDR sees what users DO. SIEM sees what admins CHANGE."

Director-Level Questions

SOC Maturity Assessment Approach

Four pillars: People (skills, staffing, retention), Process (playbooks, escalation, metrics), Technology (detection coverage, integration, automation), Governance (policies, risk alignment, compliance). "Most orgs strong in tech but underinvest in process and people."

How Do You Measure Security Program Effectiveness?

Operational: MTTD, MTTR, false positive rate. Coverage: MITRE ATT&CK heat map, endpoint coverage %. Business-aligned: Cost per alert, cost avoidance, compliance findings. "Measure what matters, not what's easy."

Security vs. Compliance

"Security is the goal — compliance is a baseline. Compliant ≠ Secure. DLP example: You can have DLP in audit mode forever and be 'compliant' but not actually preventing data loss. Compliance sets the floor — security raises the bar."

One-Liners to Remember

On consulting: "I'll learn faster seeing 10 different security programs than staying in one for 10 years."
On failure: "I own my failures and create systems to prevent recurrence."
On influence: "I influenced by demonstrating, not by demanding."
On difficult people: "Sometimes 'difficult' people are just people with constraints you don't see."
On SIEM: "It depends on the client's ecosystem — there's no universal best."
On AI security: "AI is the new shadow IT problem — enable safe adoption, don't just block."
On XDR vs SIEM: "Don't duplicate — let each tool do what it's best at."
On migration: "Migration is an opportunity to optimize, not just lift-and-shift."
On security posture: "Start with risk, not tools."

Questions to Ask the Director

Question 1 — First Months Focus

"If I'm selected for the role, what would you recommend I focus on in my first few months to be successful?"

Question 2 — Career Growth

"Beyond technical expertise, what skills or mindset do you think helped you grow from an associate level to director in consulting?"

Dangerous Questions

Salary Expectations

"I'm focused on finding the right fit. I'm comfortable within the posted range." Don't negotiate in the interview — defer to later. Never give a specific number first.

Why Are You Leaving?

Say: "Looking for variety, growth, new challenges." Never: Badmouth current employer or say "I'm bored."
