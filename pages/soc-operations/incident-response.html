Incident Response

Structured approach to handling security incidents from detection through recovery and lessons learned.

Why Incident Response Matters

Time is Everything

The difference between a minor incident and a catastrophic breach often comes down to how fast and effectively you respond.

Response SpeedTypical OutcomeCost Impact
< 1 hourContained, minimal damageLow - cleanup costs only
1-24 hoursSome spread, moderate damageMedium - some recovery needed
1-7 daysSignificant spread, major damageHigh - extensive recovery
> 7 daysFull compromise likelyCritical - may include ransom, breach notification

What Effective IR Achieves

GoalHow IR Achieves ItWithout IR...
Limit damageFast containment stops spreadAttackers expand unchecked
Preserve evidenceProper handling maintains forensic integrityEvidence destroyed, can't determine scope
Restore operationsStructured recovery minimizes downtimeChaotic recovery, longer outage
Meet obligationsProper documentation for legal/regulatoryCompliance failures, increased liability
Prevent recurrenceLessons learned improve defensesSame attack succeeds again

Incident Response Phases

The IR Lifecycle

PhaseGoalKey ActivitiesOutput
1. Preparation Be ready before incidents occur Build team, create playbooks, establish tools, practice IR plan, trained team, tested procedures
2. Detection & Analysis Identify and understand the incident Alert triage, scope assessment, evidence collection Confirmed incident, initial scope, severity
3. Containment Stop the bleeding Isolate systems, disable accounts, block IOCs Threat contained, no further spread
4. Eradication Remove the threat completely Remove malware, close access, patch vulnerabilities Threat eliminated from environment
5. Recovery Return to normal operations Restore systems, verify clean, monitor closely Systems operational, enhanced monitoring
6. Lessons Learned Improve for next time Post-incident review, documentation, improvements Incident report, action items

Incident Severity Classification

SeverityDefinitionExamplesResponse
Critical (P1) Active threat, major business impact Active ransomware, data breach in progress, critical system down All hands, 24/7, executive notification
High (P2) Confirmed compromise, significant risk Compromised admin account, malware executed, lateral movement Dedicated IR resources, management notification
Medium (P3) Potential compromise, moderate risk Suspicious activity on endpoint, phishing with click Standard investigation, SOC handles
Low (P4) Minor issue, low risk Blocked malware, policy violation, failed attack Routine handling, document and close

Containment Strategies

Containment Decision Framework

Speed vs. Completeness
Contain Fast (Short-term)Contain Carefully (Long-term)
Active threat spreadingThreat appears dormant
Data being exfiltrated nowNeed to understand full scope first
Critical systems at riskWant to preserve evidence
Simple containment availableComplex, interconnected systems

Default: When in doubt, contain fast. You can always investigate more after bleeding stops.

Containment Actions by Scenario

ScenarioContainment ActionsConsider
Compromised endpoint Network isolation, disable account used User needs device? Provide clean loaner
Compromised account Disable account, revoke sessions, reset password Service account? Plan for service disruption
Active C2 Block C2 at firewall, isolate endpoint Attacker may have backup C2 channels
Ransomware Network segment isolation, protect backups Don't power off - may lose keys in memory
Data exfiltration Block destination, disable source access May be too late - focus on stopping more

IR Decision Scenarios

What If...Then...Why
Business refuses containment? Document the risk clearly, escalate to CISO/executives for decision Business may accept risk, but decision must be documented
Scope keeps expanding? Consider broader containment (segment isolation), get more resources Better to over-contain than chase an expanding breach
Attack happened weeks ago? Focus on current scope, assume significant access achieved Attacker had time to achieve goals; prioritize understanding impact
Press/public finds out? Engage PR/Legal immediately, prepare statement, don't speculate Uncontrolled messaging creates additional damage
Law enforcement wants to investigate? Coordinate through Legal, preserve evidence, may delay recovery LE has different priorities; balance with business recovery
Team is exhausted (multi-day incident)? Rotate staff, bring in external help, prioritize rest Exhausted responders make mistakes; sustained response requires rotation
