Business Email Compromise (BEC) Investigation

Investigating email-based financial fraud - the most costly cyber attack type for businesses.

Why This Investigation Matters

BEC: The Most Expensive Cyber Attack
StatisticValueContext
Total losses (2023)$2.9 billionMore than ransomware + all other fraud combined
Average loss per incident$125,000Often no technical breach required
Recovery rate<30%Wire transfers are hard to reverse
Time to detectOften after money sentVictim discovers too late

Why BEC is Different

Unlike Other Attacks...BEC...This Means...
Ransomware breaks thingsBEC tricks peopleSocial engineering, not technical exploitation
Malware leaves forensic evidenceMay be just emailsLimited technical indicators
Data breaches take timeWire transfer takes minutesSpeed is everything for recovery
Many attacks are automatedBEC is often human-operatedAttackers adapt to your responses

Types of BEC Attacks

TypeHow It WorksDetection FocusExample
CEO Fraud Impersonate executive, request urgent wire Spoofed sender, urgency language, unusual request "I need you to wire $50K immediately. I'm in a meeting, can't call."
Account Compromise Actually compromise exec email, send from real account Impossible travel, inbox rules, unusual activity Real CFO account sends wire request (attacker controlling)
Vendor Impersonation Spoof vendor, request payment to new account Similar domains, banking change requests "Our bank account changed. Please update for next payment."
Invoice Fraud Send fake invoices from lookalike domain Lookalike domains, unusual invoice patterns Invoice from "vendor-company.com" vs "vendor-c0mpany.com"
Payroll Diversion Request direct deposit change to attacker account HR/payroll phishing, email rule changes "Please update my direct deposit to this new account."

Investigation Decision Framework

Was Money Actually Sent?

Critical First Question
StatusImmediate ActionTimeline
Yes, money was sent STOP INVESTIGATION. Call bank immediately to attempt recall. Every minute counts. First 24-48 hours critical for recovery
Pending/Processing Cancel transaction if possible. Then investigate. ACH can often be cancelled within 24 hours
No, caught before payment Proceed with investigation. Document for prevention. Standard investigation timeline

Attack Vector Determination

EvidenceAttack TypeInvestigation Focus
Email from different domain (similar but not same)Impersonation/SpoofingAnalyze headers, block domain, warn others
Email from actual internal accountAccount CompromiseFull account compromise investigation
Email from vendor's actual domainVendor CompromiseNotify vendor, they have breach
Phone call requesting actionVishing + BECCheck for email compromise enabling call

How to Investigate

Step 1: Analyze the Email

CheckWhat to Look ForRed Flag
From address vs Display nameDisplay name may say "CEO" but address is externalMismatch between name and domain
Reply-To addressMay be different from From addressReply goes to attacker, not who you think
Domain analysisCompare to legitimate domainLookalike: company.com vs c0mpany.com
Email headersOriginating IP, SPF/DKIM/DMARC resultsFailed authentication, suspicious origin
Language/toneCompare to normal communication styleUnusual urgency, secrecy requests, grammar issues
Request itselfIs this normal for this person to request?CEO doesn't usually email AP directly for wires

Step 2: Check for Account Compromise

// KQL - Check if sender account is compromised
let sender = "ceo@company.com";
SigninLogs
| where TimeGenerated > ago(7d)
| where UserPrincipalName =~ sender
| where RiskLevelDuringSignIn in ("high", "medium") or Location !in ("expected locations")
| project TimeGenerated, IPAddress, Location, RiskLevelDuringSignIn, ResultType

// KQL - Check for inbox rules (persistence for BEC)
CloudAppEvents
| where Timestamp > ago(30d)
| where AccountId =~ sender
| where ActionType in ("New-InboxRule", "Set-InboxRule", "UpdateInboxRules")
| project Timestamp, ActionType, RawEventData

// KQL - Check emails sent by this account
EmailEvents
| where Timestamp > ago(7d)
| where SenderFromAddress =~ sender
| where Subject has_any ("wire", "transfer", "payment", "invoice", "urgent", "confidential")
| project Timestamp, Subject, RecipientEmailAddress, DeliveryAction

Step 3: Identify Scope of Attack

QuestionHow to AnswerWhy It Matters
How many people received the BEC email?Search by subject/sender in email logsMultiple targets = campaign
Did anyone else take action?Check for replies, forwarding, additional paymentsMay be multiple victims
How long was attacker in environment?Check account compromise timelineLonger = more potential damage
What data did attacker access?Email access logs, file accessMay inform future attacks

What If Scenarios

What If...Then...Rationale
Money was sent days ago?Still contact bank. File IC3 report. Money sometimes traced even later.Always try, some recoveries happen weeks later
CEO is on vacation and unreachable?Contact alternate exec, don't wait. Better to pause payment than send to attacker.Attackers often strike when targets unavailable
The email passed all security checks?If account compromised, email IS from legitimate account. Focus on account security.Technical controls can't stop compromised accounts
Finance already sent multiple wires?Try to recall all. Calculate total loss. Full incident response.Each wire is separate recall attempt
Vendor's email was actually compromised (not ours)?Notify vendor immediately. They need to know. This protects their other customers.You're doing them a favor alerting them
Attacker is responding to our investigation emails?They're still in the account. Disable account immediately.Real-time attacker monitoring investigation

Escalation

BEC = Automatic Escalation

Any confirmed BEC attempt requires notification to:

  • Security leadership (CISO)
  • Legal/General Counsel
  • CFO/Finance leadership
  • If money lost: Executive team, Board if significant
